About mcp-verify

check who can do what on your MCP server, as every user you have

Imagine you built an MCP server for your SaaS. A guest connects an AI agent and asks, "what issues do we have?" The server answers. Politely. With the issues of every customer in the system.

Nothing crashed. The call returned 200. Your test said a guest may list issues, and the guest listed issues.

mcp-verify is the thing that catches this. You tell it who your users are: a guest, a member, an admin, an admin of another company. It logs in as each of them, calls your real server, and compares what happened with what you wrote down. Then it reads what came back, not only whether the call worked.

Look at the listissues row. Every cell says allow, and a table of allowed and denied would call that a pass. The ✗ is there because the response carried the other tenants' issues, and mcp-verify found their marker in it. (This is real output, trimmed a little. It comes from the demo server in this repo, started with a planted bug.)

- A call that should be refused and works. - A response that carries another company's data, even when the call was allowed. - A field that should be hidden for a role and is right there in the JSON. - A token that should bounce (expired, wrong audience, wrong scope, no token at all) and gets in. - A tool that nobody wrote a policy for. - An admin who is refused something a member can do. - Anything it can't decide. It tells you. It never calls that a pass.
